OpenAI recently published a piece on Forward Deployed Engineering, describing how their teams work directly inside real enterprise environments to bring AI systems into production. The interesting part is not only the AI angle. It is the engineering model itself.

The role sits close to the customer, close to the business problem and close to the actual implementation.

For the last 7+ years, I have worked in a similar overlap. Not with the same title, but with a very similar shape of work: understanding real user needs, translating business and product goals into engineering direction, working through constraints and helping teams ship reliable mobile, AI, accessibility and smart-glasses experiences.

This kind of engineering creates a lot of value because it is not limited to “building what is written in the ticket.”
A lot of important product work starts before the ticket exists.

> FDE teams operate in high-ambiguity environments where traditional software approaches break down.  - [Forward deployed engineering at OpenAI \| OpenAI](https://openai.com/business/the-openai-deployment-company/)

The client may not know how to describe the technical requirement.  
The business team may know the outcome they want, but not the tradeoffs involved.  
The product team may understand the user pain, but not the implementation risk.  
The engineering team may see the architecture clearly, but not the commercial urgency.

Someone has to sit in the middle and translate.
That translation is not soft work. It is engineering work.

It means taking messy input and turning it into a product direction. It means understanding constraints early enough that they do not become surprises later. It means knowing when to prototype, when to generalize, when to say no and when to take a shortcut because the learning is worth it.

This is especially true in AI products.

AI systems do not become useful just because a model is powerful. They become useful when they are deployed into real workflows, with real users, real failure modes, real privacy concerns, real latency constraints, real support issues and real business expectations.

The future of engineering is not only about writing more code faster. AI will keep improving that part.

The harder and more durable skill is knowing what should be built, why it matters, how it fits into the user’s world, and how to turn that understanding into a working product.

Forward deployed engineering gives a name to that kind of work. **And I think we will see more of it.** Not just at OpenAI. Not just in enterprise AI. But anywhere the gap between business needs and product development is too large to be handled by handoffs alone. The engineers who can bridge that gap will create disproportionate value.
